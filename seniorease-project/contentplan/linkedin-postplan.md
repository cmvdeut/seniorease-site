### Vrijdag 11 juli 08:30 — Post 3: Why no account is required

**Focus:** Library / UX-keuze

```
Every version of SeniorEase Library started with an account system. Sign up, log in, sync your data.

I removed it.

For someone who's already unsure about technology, "create an account" is often where they give up. So the app just works — scan a book, see your collection, done.

Less secure in the traditional sense. Much more usable for the people I'm actually building for.
```

## Week 2 (14–18 juli)

### Maandag 14 juli 08:30 — Post 4: A concrete AI-as-helper moment

**Focus:** AI + schrijven

```
A small example of what "using AI practically" looks like for me:

I couldn't tell if an explanation I'd written was actually simple, or just simple to me. So I asked Claude to read it as a first-time smartphone user would, and point out every word that assumes prior knowledge.

It found six. I would have found zero — I already knew what I meant.

That's the kind of help I'm looking for. Not "write this for me." More like: catch what I can't see anymore.
```

---

### Woensdag 16 juli 12:00 — Post 5: Why explanations, not features, come first

**Focus:** Filosofie SeniorEase

```
Most tech products lead with features. SeniorEase leads with explanations.

Before I add anything new, I ask: can someone who's never touched this before follow it, one step at a time, without feeling stupid?

If the answer is no, the feature waits.

It's slower. I think it's also the only way this actually helps the people it's for.
```

Building SeniorEase part-time, alongside a full-time job, I keep noticing the same thing: the hardest part isn't the technology.

## Week 3 (21 juli)

### Maandag 21 juli 08:30 — Post 7: De websitekant (seniorease.nl)

**Focus:** Website  
**Comment na publish:** https://seniorease.nl

```
The app is the newest part of SeniorEase. The website is where it started, and it's still the part I care most about.

It's step-by-step explanations of everyday technology — WhatsApp, Wi-Fi, video calling, online safety — written for people who are curious, not people who already know half of it.

No feature here is impressive on its own. A calculator with big buttons. A birthday reminder. Plain-language articles.

But together, they answer the actual question I hear most: "Is this something I can try, or will I break it?"

The answer I want people to walk away with is always: you can try it. You won't break it.
```
